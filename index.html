Filters and trend sensitivity

Filters. They decide whether the cards show LONG ✓ or ✗ and SHORT ✓ or ✗. "Strong" means a confirmed trend with a strength of 60 or more. The confirm-on-close switch trades a little delay (up to one candle) for signals that do not flicker. The confirmation counter (0 to 4) counts how many of impulse, volume, buying pressure and trend agree with the idea.

Trend sensitivity
What is trend sensitivity?

It sets how quickly the Trend indicator decides that a trend has started or ended. The indicator turns five slow checks (EMA 20/50, EMA 50/100, price vs EMA 100, EMA 50 slope, Supertrend) into a score from -100 to +100. Sensitivity controls three things: how much that score is smoothed, how strong it must be to start a trend, and for how many candles in a row it must stay there (confirmation).

ModeSmoothingStarts a trend atConfirmationCharacter
Fast3 candles±301 candleEarly signals, more false starts
Balanced5 candles±402 candlesDefault compromise
Strict10 candles±40, ADX above 183 candlesFewest false starts, reacts later

On test data with known trends, Strict gave about half the false trend starts of Fast but needed roughly 8 candles more to notice a real one. It changes everything that depends on the trend: the colour of the trend line, the trend filter, alerts, phase, reversal risk and the outlook. Use Fast on short horizons and Strict on Mid or Long. The Trend quality table under each chart compares the modes on that coin.

How it works

Open the Guide tab for what every indicator means and a step-by-step strategy. Heat Index is a 0–100 composite of Stochastic, RSI, MACD, EMA and Bollinger %B. Near 0 means oversold (buy zone, ≤ 25), near 100 means overheated (sell zone, ≥ 75). Short = 15m + 1h, Mid = 4h + 1d, Long = 1d + 1w. Trend is one smoothed stochastic line: green in an uptrend, red in a downtrend. Its direction comes from five slow checks (EMA20/50, EMA50/100, price vs EMA100, EMA50 slope, Supertrend) scaled by ADX strength, and it changes only after the signal is confirmed. No indicator is free of false signals; this one trades some delay for fewer of them. Zones that go against the confirmed trend are flagged and skipped by alerts while the trend filter is on. This is an analysis tool, not financial advice. In-page alerts run only while this page is open; use the server for push when it is closed.

Risk settings

How the size is chosen. The stop distance sets the size: position = risk amount / stop distance. The leverage is the smallest that keeps the margin within 25% of your capital, capped by your maximum and by the level where liquidation would be closer than twice the stop distance. When Bitcoin's safety is Caution the risk is halved, when it is Danger it is cut to a quarter, and counter-trend setups always use half.

Grades on history

Do the grades mean anything? This runs the grading of the best trades on the past candles of the biggest coins of this market and walks every offered trade forward to its stop and take-profits. Then it compares the results of grade A, B and C with the trades that were not offered (score under 50). It is a simplified check: one pair of timeframes of the chosen horizon; the plan is a stop at 2 ATR, TP1 at 1.5R (half closed, the stop of the rest moves to the entry) and TP2 at 3R; the fee is 0.2%; there is no funding, slippage, horizon agreement, volume spike or strength against Bitcoin; and only today's biggest coins are tested, which favours coins that kept growing. About the last half year for Mid, longer for Long. The past does not promise the future.

What this list is. Every coin is scored from 0 to 100 on trend, entry timing, exhaustion, confirmation, volume and pressure, and reward to risk. Only coins that pass the filters and score 50 or more appear, best first. LONG and SHORT, the leverage and the position size are suggestions from rules, not predictions. Paper trade them first.

Paper trades

Simulated futures trades. No order is ever sent to an exchange. Prices come from the exchange, results include a 0.05% fee per side, isolated margin and an approximate liquidation price. Funding payments are not included. Take-profit 1 closes part of the position and moves the stop to break-even; a candle that touches both the stop and a target counts as a stop-out.

What this page shows. A separate health check of the market leader, because most coins follow Bitcoin. Safety score (0 to 100, higher is safer) blends volatility (35%), market stress (30%), reversal risk (20%) and trend clarity (15%). Guide

What every indicator means, how to read it, and a step-by-step strategy. Short hints also sit next to the controls and under each chart. Use the ? button at the top to hide them.

Start in three steps

  1. Choose an exchange, a quote (BTC or USDT) and a horizon. Mid is a good start.
  2. Sort by Likely to rise or Reversal risk. Each card shows Heat, Trend, Market phase, Reversal risk, Bulls vs Bears, and the distance to support and resistance.
  3. Tap a card to open the chart. The summary block on top gives a plain verdict and a take-profit plan.

What each indicator means

Short, Mid, Long
The same coin seen on different timeframes. Short = 15m and 1h candles (hours), Mid = 4h and 1d (days), Long = 1d and 1w (weeks). Heat blends the two timeframes 40 / 60. Trend, phase, risk, outlook, support and resistance come from the slower timeframe of the pair (1h, 1d, 1w).
Heat Index, 0 to 100
How stretched the price is, from Stochastic, RSI, MACD, EMA and Bollinger %B combined. Up to 25 is the buy zone (oversold), from 75 the sell zone (overheated). It does not say where the trend goes: a strong uptrend can stay overheated for weeks, which is why the trend filter exists.
Trend line
One smoothed stochastic line from 0 to 100. Green = confirmed uptrend, red = confirmed downtrend, yellow = no clear trend. Triangles mark a confirmed change. Trade with the colour, do not buy a red line.
Trend sensitivity
How fast the trend indicator changes its mind (Fast, Balanced, Strict). Stricter means fewer false trend starts but later signals. See the explanation under the control on the Market tab.
Trend filter
Flags buy zones that appear inside a confirmed downtrend and sell zones inside a confirmed uptrend ("against the trend") and skips alerts for them.
Reversal risk, 0 to 100
How tired the current confirmed trend looks. It combines stretch (Heat), divergences, fading momentum (ADX and MACD), a decaying trend score, distance from EMA 20 and the Bollinger band, and a shift of pressure from one side to the other. Below 30 low, 30 to 60 moderate, 60 to 80 high, above 80 very high. It is a score, not a probability. The table "Reversal risk calibration" under the chart shows, for that coin and timeframe, how often the trend actually ended afterwards.
Divergences
Price and an oscillator (RSI, MACD or Stochastic) disagree. Bearish: price makes a higher high, the oscillator a lower high, a top may be forming. Bullish: price makes a lower low, the oscillator a higher low, a bottom may be forming. Hidden bullish / hidden bearish are the opposite pattern and suggest the trend will continue. Two or more oscillators agreeing counts more. They are confirmed three candles after the swing, so they never repaint.
Bulls vs Bears
Share of buying against selling pressure (volume inside each candle plus +DI / -DI). Above 50 buyers lead. In an uptrend a falling bull share is an early warning.
Market phase
Markup: confirmed uptrend. Markdown: confirmed downtrend. Distribution: sideways after a rise, near the highs, a top may be forming. Accumulation: sideways after a fall, near the lows, a base may be forming. Range: no trend and no prior trend. "Late" means the risk is already high.
Support and resistance
Price zones where the price turned at least twice. Support sits below the price, resistance above. A zone with more touches is stronger. Zones are targets for taking profit and places for stops.
Regression channel
A straight line fitted through the visible candles with a band of two standard deviations. The slope is the trend, R² says how clean it is, and the edges mark stretched prices. Price tends to return to the middle line. Zoom changes the length.
Outlook, -100 to +100
The expected bias of the next move. In a trend it is the direction times how little exhaustion there is: a healthy uptrend scores high, an exhausted uptrend scores low or negative. "Likely to rise" and "Likely to fall" sort by it. Without a trend it is a small bias from pressure and Heat.
Take-profit signals and zones
In an uptrend, TP1 fires when the reversal risk crosses 60 or a bearish divergence appears (take part of the position). TP2 fires when the risk crosses 80, two oscillators show a divergence, or the uptrend ends (take most or all). The shaded band marks the stretch where risk stayed at 60 or more. In a downtrend the same signals are marked as "bottom watch".
Horizon confluence
Short, Mid and Long Heat are in the same zone at once: all three in the buy zone, or all three in the sell zone. Such moments are rarer than a single-horizon signal and carry more weight. They get a ★ badge on the card, a filter in the list, a bonus in the Setups score and an option to alert only on them.
Volume spike
The 24h volume of the coin against the average daily volume of the previous 7 days, shown as ×3.2. An abnormal volume (×2 or more) makes a signal more believable, because real trading stands behind the move. It is not the same as Candle vol, which compares the last candle with the previous 20 candles.
Funding rate and open interest
Funding is the payment between longs and shorts of a perpetual future. A strongly positive funding means crowded longs: together with a sell zone it makes a sell signal stronger. A negative funding means crowded shorts: together with a buy zone it makes a buy signal stronger (marked with ✓). Open interest is the size of all open positions. The data comes from the public futures endpoints of the exchange.
Exchange comparison
On the chart page the same coin is shown on every exchange and quote with the price difference to the cheapest one. A spread of 0.3% or more is highlighted. Small differences are normal; a large one can mean a thin market or a delayed price.
Alert settings
Cooldown allows one alert per coin every X hours. Quiet hours silence alerts overnight. The zone thresholds change where the buy and sell zones start. The option "only on horizon confluence" sends an alert only when all three horizons agree. The same rules are applied by the push server.
Live prices
For Binance the prices update through a WebSocket stream (a green dot next to the update time), so the list changes about once per second without polling. Closed candles of the open chart also arrive by stream. Bybit and OKX have no public all-market stream, so they keep the light polling every few seconds.
Overbought and oversold
Overbought means the price has risen fast and far, so a pause or a pullback becomes more likely; oversold means the opposite. A card is labelled when Heat is in the sell or buy zone, or RSI is at 70 or above (30 or below). When Heat and RSI both agree the label is "strongly". On the chart the OB/OS layer shades the stretches where Heat was overbought (red) or oversold (green), and the RSI, Stochastic and Heat panels name their zones. These are warnings about stretch, not orders to sell or buy: in a strong trend the price can stay overbought for a long time.
Altcoin dominance
Dominance is the share of a group of coins in the total crypto market cap. BTC.D is Bitcoin's share, ETH.D Ethereum's, and Alts.D everything except Bitcoin and the stablecoins (it includes ETH). The numbers come from CoinGecko and are refreshed every ten minutes; this page keeps its own history, so the 24h and 7d changes appear after it has been open for a while. Next to it the app shows an index from your exchange: how many of the 50 largest BTC pairs beat Bitcoin over 7, 30 and 90 days. Bitcoin rising with falling dominance points to an altcoin season; Bitcoin falling with rising dominance means altcoins are hit harder. The block sits on the BTC/USDT tab, with a summary line above the Market list.
Turn confirmation and signal age
A coin in the buy zone is not a buy yet: the scale may still be falling, and buying then means catching a falling knife. A turn is confirmed when Heat was in the zone during the last six candles and has started to leave it (at least four points, still moving that way). The card shows Waiting for a turn (in the zone, not confirmed), Confirmed buy or sell (the turn is confirmed on the faster timeframe of the horizon) and Strong buy or sell (a confirmed turn together with a confluence of all three horizons). The age counts candles of the faster timeframe since the confirmation (or since the zone was entered): fresh is up to 3 candles, aging up to 12, old beyond that. The filter "Only confirmed turns" hides coins that are still falling.
30-day range
The distance from the price to the lowest low and the highest high of the last 30 daily candles, with a small bar that shows where the price sits between them (left is near the low, right near the high). Near the low together with a confirmed turn is a classic place to look for a bounce; near the high together with an overbought label is a place to be careful.
Strength against Bitcoin
For BTC pairs it is the pair's own performance; for USDT pairs the coin's move is compared with Bitcoin's move. Steady means ahead of Bitcoin over both 7 and 30 days, so the strength is not just a one-week bounce. Bounce: only this week is ahead. Fading: ahead over 30 days but behind this week. Lagging: behind on both. The Setups score adds or removes a few points for it, and the filter "Only coins that beat Bitcoin" keeps the steady ones.
Table
An alternative to the cards: one row per coin with the main numbers, convenient on a tablet or a wide screen. It uses the same filters and search as the Market tab. Tap a title to sort, tap a row to open the chart.
Portfolio
Enter the coin, the price at which you bought it and, optionally, the quantity. The tab shows the result now in percent and in BTC (for USDT pairs also in USDT), the value of the position, and the same labels as on the cards (zone, overbought or oversold, take-profit zone). Positions are stored only in this browser.
Indicator settings
In Settings you can change the zone thresholds (25 / 75, 20 / 80, 30 / 70 or your own numbers) and the periods of RSI, Stochastic, MACD, the two EMAs, Bollinger Bands and ATR, and the RSI overbought and oversold levels. Shorter periods react faster and give more false signals. The trend indicator keeps its fixed settings.
Price chart inside a card
Every card has a spoiler "Price chart". Open it to see the candles of that coin with the two EMA lines and the support and resistance levels, without leaving the list. The buttons 15m, 1h, 4h and 1d change the candle size, the two lens buttons zoom, and the link opens the full chart with all the indicators. The candles are loaded only when the spoiler is open, and it stays open between visits.
Time in the zone
Next to the Buy zone or Sell zone badge the card shows how long the Heat of the chosen horizon has been in that zone, for example "Buy zone · 12 h". It is counted on the same scale the card shows (the blend of the horizon's two timeframes), from the candle that put it in the zone. A plus sign ("5+ d") means the loaded history starts inside the zone, so the real time is longer. Touch the badge to see since when. A fresh entry is a different situation from a coin that has sat in the zone for days: the longer it stays, the more the move is already behind it, and the more it matters whether Heat has started to turn.
Market temperature
At the top of the Market tab: the average Heat of all coins of the chosen market and horizon. 0 means everything is oversold (a very cold market), 100 means everything is overheated (a hot market). Under the thermometer you see how many coins are in the buy zone, in the sell zone and in between, how many are in an uptrend and how many in a downtrend, and the same temperature for the Short, Mid and Long horizons. The change against 24 hours ago appears once this page has collected that much history. A cold market is a place to look for bounces, a hot one a place to be careful; it works best together with the trend of Bitcoin.
Candlestick patterns
Short shapes of one to three candles that traders read as a sign of a turn or a pause: hammer, shooting star, bullish and bearish engulfing, morning and evening star, three white soldiers, three black crows and doji. The app marks them with letters on the chart and in the card, but only when the move before them fits (a hammer matters after a decline), and it sizes them against ATR so small noise does not count. A pattern is a weak signal on its own: look at support, volume and the trend. The Signal accuracy table shows how each one performed on your data.
Double bottom and double top
Two lows (or two highs) of about the same price with a clear swing between them, a W or an M. The pattern counts only after the price closes beyond that swing, the neckline: until then it is only "forming" and is not used in the statistics. The dotted line is the measured target, the neckline plus the height of the pattern.
Fibonacci levels
Levels at 23.6%, 38.2%, 50%, 61.8% and 78.6% of the biggest recent swing, plus the 127.2% and 161.8% extensions. They show where a pullback often stops. The layer is off by default; the card shows a chip when the price is within 3% of a retracement level.
Volume profile
How much was traded at each price of the visible candles. The orange bar is the point of control, the busiest price; the dotted lines mark the value area that holds 70% of the volume. Prices with a lot of volume often act as support or resistance, and prices with little volume are crossed quickly. It needs volume data and is off by default.
Golden cross and death cross
The 50-candle average crossing above (golden) or below (death) the 200-candle average. Classic signs of a lasting trend, but they react late: they mostly confirm a move that has already happened. The card shows how long ago the cross was, the chart marks it with a GOLDEN or DEATH tag.
Trend strength (ADX)
ADX measures how strong a trend is, not its direction: below 20 there is no trend worth following, 20 to 25 a trend is emerging, 25 to 40 it is strong, 40 to 50 very strong, above 50 extreme. The arrow on the card is the direction (+DI against -DI), and a second arrow means ADX is rising. The ADX panel on the chart shows the three lines.
Squeeze
The word Squeeze appears when the Bollinger Bands are narrowing, that is when volatility is falling, and in bold when the width is among the lowest 15% of the last 120 candles. A quiet market often precedes a larger move, but the squeeze does not say in which direction: wait for the breakout.
Stochastic or Stochastic RSI
Heat uses the regular Stochastic (14, 3). Stochastic RSI applies the same formula to RSI, so it reaches the extremes more often and turns earlier: more signals, more false ones. Neither is better in general. The Signal accuracy table on the Market tab compares the two on your data (the rows "leaves oversold" and "leaves overbought"), so you can see which one worked for the coins and the timeframe you use.
Signal accuracy
At the top of the Market tab the app checks its own signals on the loaded candles: for every divergence, pattern, formation, cross and oscillator signal it looks at the price 5, 10 or 20 candles later. Hit rate is how often the price moved the promised way; edge is that minus how often it moves that way anyway. Only a clearly positive edge on 30 or more cases is worth trusting, and results on a few weeks of candles can change with the market.
Direction of Bitcoin dominance
The arrow next to BTC.D shows which way the dominance moved over 24 hours: up (orange), down (blue) or flat. Until the page has collected a day of history the value is estimated from Bitcoin's move against the whole market and marked with a star.
Screenshot of a coin card
On every card and on the chart page there is a camera button. It draws a minimal picture of the coin on your device: price with the 24 hour and 7 day changes, the zone and the time in it, signals and formations, the price line with support and resistance, Heat of the three horizons, up to twelve figures (trend, ADX, risk, phase, bulls and bears, strength against BTC, 30-day range, volume, RSI, levels, funding, liquidations), the summary, the history of the signals and the market context. The preview has a light and a dark theme. You can save the image, share it (where the browser allows) or copy it. Nothing is uploaded anywhere.
Sorting, filters and presets
Besides the sort buttons there is a list with every sort key (more than thirty), a direction button and a second key that breaks ties. "More filters" narrows the list by zone, time in the zone, signal, trend, ADX, phase, reversal risk, 24h change, volume spike, strength against Bitcoin, position in the 30-day range, a recent golden or death cross, funding, RSI, Stochastic, squeeze, patterns, starred coins and the top by volume. All the filters work together. Presets set filters and sorting in one tap, and you can save your own (up to 12). The active filters are shown above the coins with a cross to remove each one. The Table tab uses the same filters.
Futures and leverage
A chip in the card shows whether the coin has a perpetual futures contract on the exchange of the card and the leverage range. Bybit and OKX publish the range; Binance gives leverage only to signed requests, so there the chip only says that futures exist. The tooltip lists all three exchanges. "Spot only" means no contract was found anywhere, "No futures here" that another exchange has one. The Futures filter and the Max leverage sort key help to find coins you can short. In the best trades the leverage is capped by the coin's maximum, spot-only coins get no leverage, and a SHORT on them is marked as not possible and loses 10 points. The range is for the smallest position and falls as the position grows; the data comes from the public lists of the exchanges and is kept for 12 hours.
Chance of a pattern
When you touch or hover over a pattern, a formation, a cross or a divergence on the chart, the tooltip and the line under the chart show its chance to work: the share of past cases where the price moved the expected way a few candles later, next to the share of candles where it moves that way anyway. It is counted on all coins of the market in the loaded candles (and on this coin alone), for the number of candles chosen in the Signal accuracy block. With fewer than 30 cases it is only a hint.
Screenshot of the chart
Chart image: the button above the chart makes a picture of the chart as it is on the screen, with the pair and the price on top. In the preview choose the price only, the price with volume or all the panels, and the light or the dark theme.

Fear and greed
A mood index of the whole crypto market from 0 (extreme fear) to 100 (extreme greed), made by alternative.me, with yesterday, 7 days ago and 30 days ago. It sits under the market temperature. Extremes often come near turning points, but the mood can stay extreme for weeks, so it is context, not a signal. If the service cannot be reached the card says so, and the last known value is shown marked as old.
Liquidity of a trade
Every best trade shows what share of the 24h volume of the coin the position makes. Under 0.05% is fine, under 0.25% acceptable, under 1% thin, more is illiquid, and the plan warns from 0.25%. The button Check the order book reads the real order book of the exchange and shows the slippage a market order of this size would have on each side and how much value stands within 1% of the price. A small coin with a large position can move its own price: reduce the size or use limit orders.
Grades on history
In Best trades, Grades on history grades past candles of the biggest coins and walks every offered trade forward to its stop and take-profits, then compares grade A, B and C with the trades that were not offered (score under 50). The result is the average in R with a 95% interval; with fewer than 30 trades a group is only a hint, and the verdict says better, worse, no clear difference or too few. It is a simplified check (one pair of timeframes, a stop at 2 ATR, TP1 at 1.5R with half closed and the stop moved to the entry, TP2 at 3R, a 0.2% fee, no funding, slippage, horizon agreement, volume spike or strength against Bitcoin) on about the last half year of today's biggest coins, which favours coins that kept growing. A good result does not promise the future; a bad one is a reason to distrust the grades.
Where to stand
The card under the market temperature answers where it is least exposed to Bitcoin's moves right now: in USDT (cash), in Bitcoin or in altcoins. The marker sits on a scale from USDT through Bitcoin to altcoins. It comes from a score of Bitcoin's state: the daily trend (up to ±40), the 4h trend (±10), overheating (up to −10), exhaustion of the trend (up to −10), Bitcoin safety (Caution −10, Danger −20), a warning about a sharp move (−10) and greed in the market (up to −8). Below −20 the zone is USDT; from +30 it is altcoins, but only when at least half of the biggest alts held up against Bitcoin over 14 days and Bitcoin's dominance is not rising; otherwise it is Bitcoin. Confidence is low when a sharp move is expected or the alts are not loaded yet. Under the scale the app measures, on the daily candles of up to 24 of the biggest ALT/BTC pairs of your exchange, how the alts moved on days when BTC jumped or fell (3% or more, 1 to 3%, or flat): in dollars and against Bitcoin, how often the pairs fell, and the beta of every alt. Warnings appear when Bitcoin's volatility is unusually low (a squeeze, which often comes before a sharp move) or when it is moving violently, and they quote the numbers of that study: whether the ALT/BTC pairs lost value on days of a sharp rally and whether the alts fell harder than Bitcoin on days of a sharp fall. The study covers the last 300 days and can change. Safest here means least exposed, not safe: USDT also carries the risk of the issuer, the exchange and a lost peg. It is a rule-based reading, not advice.

Strategy: Trend + Risk (long only, spot)

The idea: buy pullbacks inside a healthy uptrend and sell into exhaustion, instead of guessing tops and bottoms. Every step uses indicators you can see on the card or the chart.

  1. Choose the horizon. Mid for swing trades (days), Short for day trades, Long for positions held for weeks.
  2. Find candidates. Sort by Likely to rise. Keep coins with Trend up, Phase Markup, Reversal risk below 35, Bulls above 50, and "TFs agree" on the trend line.
  3. Wait for a pullback. Enter when Heat on the faster timeframe falls to 35 or lower (best 25 or lower) near a support zone or the lower edge of the regression channel. A hidden bullish divergence is a bonus. Skip the coin if a bearish divergence is active.
  4. Place the stop just below the nearest support (or about 2 x ATR). Size the position so that hitting the stop loses at most 1% of your capital. Never go all-in.
  5. Take profit in steps. At TP1 (risk 60 or a bearish divergence) sell about half and move the stop to break-even. At TP2 (risk 80, two-oscillator divergence, or the trend ends) sell the rest. The next resistance and the upper channel edge are natural targets in between.
  6. While holding. Risk 35 to 60: hold, do not add, trail the stop under the latest swing low. If the Trend turns sideways or down (confirmed) or Phase becomes Distribution, leave even if no target was hit.
  7. Counter-trend entries (advanced, half size). Only when a downtrend shows reversal risk of 75 or more, a bullish divergence on at least two oscillators, price at a support or the lower channel edge, and bulls gaining. Do not add until the trend line leaves red and a confirmed change appears.
  8. Avoid. Buying in Markdown, chasing a Heat above 75, thin coins (check the volume), moving the stop away, and trading on a single indicator.

Check it before you trust it

  • Signal history, Reversal risk calibration, Divergence results and Trend quality sit under every chart. They are measured on that coin and timeframe, with the history loaded.
  • The Simulator tab replays the Heat strategy and the Trend + Risk strategy on real recent candles, with fees and slippage, next to buy and hold.
  • Treat numbers from fewer than about 10 signals as noise.

Limits

These indicators are rules of thumb built from past prices. They do not predict the future, a risk of 80 is not an 80% chance of a reversal, and every setting trades speed for false signals. News, listings and sudden liquidations move prices in ways no chart indicator sees. This is an analysis tool, not financial advice.

How it works. Signals are read at the close of a candle and executed at the open of the next one, so there is no peeking into the future. Each buy and sell pays the fee and slippage you set. The stop-loss sells if the candle's low touches the stop price. A trade still open at the end is valued at the last close. Heat is the composite of Stochastic, RSI, MACD, EMA and Bollinger %B on the faster timeframe of the horizon, blended 0.4 / 0.6 with the slower timeframe (the slower timeframe is only used once its candle has closed). One month is a small sample: a few trades can swing the result either way, and past results do not predict future ones. This is an experiment, not financial advice.
